# Frontier economy: design summary

Generated by `run.ps1 report` from the catalog and the balance simulator (catalog schema 1, content 1, 606 tuning keys, tuning digest `f7b3888e49b20760`). Every table below is produced from the data, so it cannot drift from `data\*.json`. Companion files: `ATLAS_TEXT.md` (codex text), `ICON_BRIEF.md` (icon artist brief), `TUNING_KEYS.md` (every knob), `INTEGRATION.md` (how to import it into Unity), `PROGRESS.md` (build log), charts `chart_*.png`.

Contents: 34 resources, 24 recipes, 31 structures, 6 tool ladders x 7 tiers, 16 upgrades, 60 research nodes, 8 obstacle kinds, one shard economy, one score formula.

## 1. The loop in one page

1. **Start small.** The pack holds 9 slots, so the first Warehouse comes in the first minutes. Wood, stone and scrap are chopped, mined and salvaged by hand; the Refinery turns wood into fuel and planks.
2. **Fuel is the leash.** Travel costs fuel (`legFuel = 1.5 + 0.14 x ly^1.1`, one leg per Hyperspace Run, refund up to 40 % for a perfect run). Jump range is an upgrade, so early on only a handful of systems are reachable; each jump level opens a ring of the map.
3. **Deeper worlds, better tiers.** Planet tier grows with distance (`floor(1.6 x log2(1 + d/60))`); tier gates resources (iron at 1, titanium and crystal at 2, helium-3 and cores at 3, radium at 4, dark matter at 6), hazards and the hull plating needed to land.
4. **Everything advanced is built from the basics.** Steel is iron plate and planks; planks are wood; biocomposite, thermal alloy and void alloy chain down to wood, stone and scrap again. A late project therefore needs tens of thousands of tier-0 units: the player keeps returning to the starter worlds, but every tool tier (x1.0 to x12.5), camp level and megaproject bonus makes the same mini-game pay more, so the return trip is an evening, not a month.
5. **Time only on build jobs.** Gathering, refining and crafting are instant (crafting is batched per tap). Build, upgrade and clear jobs have builder timers (instant at first, a day at most for ordinary jobs, 2 to 10 days per megaproject stage). Nova Shards skip timers, buy builders and cosmetics; they never buy resources, combat power or score.
6. **A next shiny thing at every step.** The goal ladder (section 10) lists 148 headline wants; the simulator checks that a regular player always completes something or visibly advances a megaproject within the era's limit.

## 2. Decisions kept and changes against the plan

Kept (ROADMAP section 0): no attacking other planets; clutter is cleared Clash-of-Clans style (resources, short builder timer, about 5 % shard pods); timers only on build, upgrade and clear jobs; rewarded ads are opt-in; aliens come in waves the player calls by burning fuel into the Alarm Beacon.
User-directed update: shards also buy already-unlocked structures without ingredients or a builder, ready immediately.
Price is twice the full build timer's skip cost, with a 10-shard minimum. Progression and planet requirements remain.

Changes and refinements (each one is a tuning key or a data row, so it can be reversed):

- **Ids.** `alloy` becomes `steel` and `satellite` becomes `orbital_relay` (the Satellite is folded into the Orbital Relay). `LegacyIdMap` holds both plus the identity rows for every other legacy id; a test round-trips old saves.
- **Fuel is crafted only.** No deposit yields `fuel`: the Refinery makes it (8 wood -> 3, 6 biomass -> 5, 3 crystal -> 8, 3 methane -> 9, 10 scrap -> 2). The old drilled fuel is gone, so fuel always costs tier-0 volume.
- **Batch taps replace +25 % output per level.** Crafting is instant, one tap runs a whole batch (batch size by station level 1, 2, 4, 8, 14, 24, 40, 64, 100, 150), which keeps late crafting from being thousands of taps.
- **Scrap is salvaged by hand** (a free Claw mini-game on any world); gather camps scale wood, stone and the other bulk resources.
- **Storage curve.** Warehouse slots are 18 + 9 per level up to level 5, then 66, 84, 108, 138, 174 at levels 6 to 10 (the end-game answer to the small pack, with Cargo Compression multiplying stacks by 1.15 to 1.75). Without it the largest late purchases (about 900 slots of items) could not be assembled.
- **Era bands follow gates, not tiers.** Foothold = first landing on another world, Settlement = Research Lab plus jump level 1, Industry = first hazard plating, Interstellar = radiation plating plus antimatter, Ascension = megaproject stage 3. Interstellar therefore spans planet tiers 5 to 7.
- **Megaprojects are installments.** A stage is filled by contributions (the next stage can be prefunded while a timer runs), uses no builder, and its effect is stage x bonus. Stage timers are 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 days before the per-project multiplier, capped at 10 days.
- **Cost shape.** Each cost line is capped in slots (12 + 14 per tier) so a base can hold what a purchase asks; a per-tier pacing multiplier (`cost.era.0..8`) and `cost.scale.mega` (0.7) were tuned with the simulator against the era bands. Defense upgrades are reach-gated and steeper (growth 2.2).
- **Score weights retuned** (reach 700, worlds 130, industry 50 with exponent 1.6, tools 800, upgrades 300, Atlas 100/80, megaproject stage 28 000) so the simulated profiles hit the plan targets (section 14). The structure of the plan formula is unchanged.
- **Shard faucets.** Besides the plan's list the ledger has weekly objectives (3 x 5 shards, a recurring achievement) and the board pays 20 shards for an active week; everything else is as planned. Free income is about 70 shards a week long-run, 200 to 400 in the first week.
- **Autopilot** (`nav_autopilot` research) is a convenience for short sessions: no mini-game, no refund, 1.6x fuel, 12 s per leg. Casual players use it; regular and hardcore players fly the Hyperspace Run.
- **Obstacle clutter** is data (8 kinds by biome, 5 % pods, first 4 pads free, home world about 8 % blocked); the Colony Hub discounts clearing by 5 % per level above 1 (45 % at level 10).

## 8. Travel

`routeFuel = (legs x 1.5 + 0.14 x routeLy^1.1) x engineFactor x (1 + 0.2 x cargoFill) x (1 - discounts) x (autopilot 1.6)`; the route length is the straight distance x 1.15 detour, split into the fewest legs that stay below 90 % of the jump range; each leg is one Hyperspace Run (25 to 90 s) that refunds up to 40 % of its fuel by performance. ## 9. Eras, target bands and simulated times

The simulator (`SimEngine`) is a deterministic average player: sessions per day, minutes per session, a harvest run of 25 to 40 s with the `0.4 + 1.1 x quality` yield curve, tool and camp multipliers, crafting taps, builders and timers, called waves, travel to the cheapest world that offers what is missing (and back to the starter worlds when bulk volume is needed), pack and warehouse limits and a greedy upgrade policy over the goal ladder (section 10). Shard use is disabled in the base model. The same seed gives the same timeline (checked by a test and a digest).

## 12. Shards, builders and timers

**Timers.** Job time per structure level follows one shared curve (index 0 = instant, 5 s, 20 s, 1 min, 3 min, 10 min, 30 min, 1 h, 3 h, 8 h, 16 h, 24 h) shifted per structure (`time.start.*`, `time.step.*`); ordinary jobs are capped at one day and megaproject stages at 10 days. The full table is in section 5 and `data\structures.json`; tests enforce that times never fall with level and never exceed their cap.

**Builders.** 1 at the start; the second comes from the research `field_engineering`; the third and fourth from the Colony Hub (level 3 and level 7) or, as a shortcut, from shards (250 and 600). The cap is 4 whichever route is used. Megaprojects use no builder.

**Skip price.** `shards = max(1, ceil(1.2 x minutes ^ 0.75))`, free for the last 30 seconds. It depends on time only: skipping never changes what a job costs in resources, and no function converts shards to resources. The curve is monotone and concave (finishing one job never costs more than finishing it in two paid halves).

## 14. Frontier Score

Exactly the plan 8.1 formula as a pure function over `ScoreInputs` (no premium or purchase input exists; a test inspects the type), weights in `ScoreFormula` / `score.*` tuning keys:

```
Reach     R = floor(700 x sqrt(maxDistanceFromHome_ly / 10))
Worlds    W = sum over landed worlds of 130 x (1 + tier)       (full value up to 500 worlds, best tiers first, then 20 %)
Industry  I = sum over structures of 50 x level^1.6 x (1 + structureTier)
Tech      T = 300 x sum(upgradeLevels) + 800 x sum(toolTiers bought)
Defense   D = sum of cleared waves 12 x sqrt(danger) x min(1, 25 / wavesClearedOnThatWorld)   (anti-farm: nothing past 25 waves per world)
Atlas     A = 100 x resourcesDiscovered + 80 x recipesCrafted + 28000 x megaprojectStages
Score = floor((R + W + I + T + D + A) x (1 + 0.10 x ascensionRank))
```

The weights were retuned against the simulator (the plan's weights gave 12 to 14 k after one hour and about 0.5 M for a finished profile): reach 1200 -> 700, worlds 100 -> 130, industry 60 -> 50 with exponent 1.25 -> 1.6, upgrades 400 -> 300, tool tiers 1500 -> 800, Atlas 250/150/400 -> 100/80/28000. Targets: early hours 5 to 10 k, mid about 100 k, end game 1 M+.

## 16. Known risks

- **The finite content ends.** A hardcore player (2.5 h a day) ticks the last headline goal after about a year and every structure, tool and upgrade level after about 16 months. Ascension (new seeded universe, permanent bonuses), weekly boards and megaprojects beyond stage 5 must exist before the first such players arrive; the simulator does not model an Ascension cycle yet.
- **The simulated player is an average, not a person.** It follows a greedy policy over a fixed ladder; real players will wander, skip goals and optimise differently. The bands are wide on purpose (about 2.5x per milestone); use Remote Config (`cost.era.*`, `cost.scale.*`) to retune from live telemetry.
- **Storage is a real constraint** (the largest late purchases need 400 to 900 slots of items). The accelerated Warehouse curve, Cargo Compression and the Cargo Depot pooling carry it; if the UI makes managing hundreds of slots painful, raise stack sizes (`value`/stack data) rather than shrinking costs.
- **Shard prices were never tested against real buyers.** The curve is the plan's; the pack sizes and cosmetic prices are placeholders chosen so the smallest pack cannot skip a day and larger packs are better value. Free income is about 65 shards a week; if timers turn out to matter more or less than in the simulation, move `shard.skip.*` and the faucet keys.
- **Timers matter little in the simulation** (idle waiting on builders is close to zero for regular and casual players) because gathering volume is the bottleneck, as designed. Monetisation pressure must therefore come from convenience and cosmetics and the multi-day megaproject timers, not from ordinary jobs.
- **Score term balance.** Atlas via megaproject stages (about 40 % of a finished profile) and Worlds (about a third) dominate late; Defense is nearly empty in the simulation. The weights are data and the Rank screen shows the breakdown, so rebalance after the first leaderboard data.
- **Obstacles and pads are not simulated** (the simulator ignores pad counts and clutter), so remote-base build-out is slightly optimistic; the rules keep clutter cheap (a few percent of a structure's cost) to bound the error.
- **Universe generator coupling.** Resource availability uses the generator's tier formula and resource table; the Monte-Carlo test proves coverage within 576 ly for generator version 1. A generator change must rerun the coverage tests.
